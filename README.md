# Buybacks vs dividends

**Goal:** Compare how the S&P 500 split shareholder payouts between dividends and buybacks over ten years (2015-2024), and which channel actually moves when earnings or policy change.

**Macro steps:**
1. Transcribed annual dividends, buybacks, and operating earnings from S&P Dow Jones Indices buyback reports (calendar years 2015-2024, $B; see SOURCES.md).
2. Computed total payout, buyback share of payout, payout ratio vs operating earnings, and buyback-to-dividend ratio for each year (`results.csv`).
3. Compared growth behavior: 9-year CAGR and year-over-year growth volatility for each channel, plus the 2018 and 2020 episodes (`growth.csv`).
4. Built a dividend-smooth counterfactual (v2): buybacks re-grown each year at that year's dividend growth rate from the 2015 level, to separate timing from total (`counterfactual.csv`, `counterfactual.png`).
5. Measured earnings responsiveness (v3): correlation and beta of each channel's growth against operating-earnings growth, plus average growth in earnings-up vs earnings-down years (`responsiveness.csv`, `responsiveness.png`).
6. Cumulated the decade and pooled it into eras (v4): cumulative payout vs cumulative earnings with the cumulative payout ratio (`cumulative.csv`, `cumulative.png`), and era totals for 2015-2019, 2020-2022, and 2023-2024 (`eras.csv`).
7. Charted the stacked payout mix and the buyback share over time (`results.png`). A live FRED pull (series SP500) is run only as a connectivity sanity check; payout figures come from the S&P DJI reports, not FRED.

**Versions:** v1, initial analysis. v2, dividend-smooth counterfactual + growth table (actual vs smooth buybacks, annual and cumulative excess) and the first test suite (12 tests); calculations refactored into build_rows / build_growth / build_counterfactual, with `results.csv` unchanged. v3, earnings-responsiveness cut (correlation, beta, and earnings up/down-year split per channel, new build_responsiveness + scatter chart); 7 new tests (19 total), with `results.csv`, `growth.csv`, and `counterfactual.csv` unchanged. v4, cumulative / era lens (new build_cumulative + build_eras, cumulative.csv + eras.csv + cumulative.png); 7 new tests (26 total), with `results.csv`, `growth.csv`, `counterfactual.csv`, and `responsiveness.csv` unchanged.

**Short results:** Buybacks were the majority channel in all 10 years, totaling $7,225B of $12,143B paid out (59.5%); the buyback share was 59.9% in 2015 and 60.0% in 2024, dipping to 51.8% in 2020 and peaking at 63.9% in 2018. Both channels grew at the same 5.7%/yr CAGR, but they behaved very differently year to year: buyback growth volatility was 32.8 points vs 3.1 for dividends. In 2018 buybacks jumped 55.3% after the TCJA while dividends rose 8.7%; in 2020 buybacks were cut 28.7% while dividends slipped only 0.5%. Total payout hit a record $1,572B in 2024, yet the payout ratio vs operating earnings fell from 107.8% in 2015 to 79.9% in 2024 because earnings grew faster than payouts. The v2 counterfactual shows that volatility was timing, not total: a dividend-smooth path lands at $942.3B in 2024 vs $942.6B actual and pays $7,360B over the decade vs $7,225B actual (just $135B more), even though actual buybacks ran as much as $244B behind the smooth path cumulatively by end-2020 (single-year gap -$203B in 2020, +$124B in 2018). The v3 cut shows what buybacks move with: their growth tracks earnings growth almost point-for-point (beta 1.06, correlation 0.84 over the 9 growth years), while dividend growth barely responds at all (beta 0.03, correlation 0.26). In the two earnings-down years (2020, 2022) buybacks averaged -12.0% while dividends still averaged +5.0%; in earnings-up years buybacks averaged +15.8% vs dividends +6.0%. The v4 cut shows the annual swing mostly averages away: pooled by era the buyback share is 59.6% (2015-2019), 59.9% (2020-2022), and 58.8% (2023-2024) — a 1.1-point range against a 12.1-point annual range (51.8% to 63.9%) — while the era payout ratio falls steadily from 97.2% to 87.5% to 78.7% as earnings outgrow payouts. Cumulated, the decade paid out $12,143B against $13,650B of operating earnings (89.0%), down from 107.8% in 2015 alone.
